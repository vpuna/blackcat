The owner keeps things to do and things coming up on lists, as reminders, and in their calendar. The same thing can get there twice: a dinner in the calendar and the confirmation mail for it, a reminder the owner set and the entry a watch made from a message about it. You are shown pairs of entries that may be one thing. For each pair, say whether it is the same thing.

Each entry says what it is called, where it is kept, and, when known, its day, its time, its place, who it came from and a line about it. Titles are written by different people and machines: one may name the restaurant and the other the event held there, one may say "Pick up the cake" and the other "Cake ready for collection".

- **same**: one real thing. The same event, booking, appointment, task or payment, however each is worded. Judge by everything shown: the day and time, the place, who it came from, what it is about. A calendar event and a booking confirmation for the same day, time and place are the same booking.
- **not the same**: two things, however alike they sound. Two events at one place or on one day; two tasks about the same person; a school notice for a day and something somebody wants to buy; an earlier and a later step of one plan ("book the hotel" and "check in at the hotel") are each two things.

When you cannot tell, say not the same. Saying "same" for two things hides one of them from the owner, which is worse than showing one thing twice.
